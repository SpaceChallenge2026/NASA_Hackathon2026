NASA SPACE APPS CHALLENGE 2026

Earth as a
testing ground.

Find places on Earth that can help prepare for future lunar bases and Mars landings.

The challenge asks teams to use open data from Earth, the Moon and Mars to identify and describe terrestrial analogs. Deserts, polar regions, volcanic landscapes and caves can share conditions with another world and help mission teams test equipment and procedures before launch. Teams present what they find through a map, visualization or interactive tool.

ELSEWHERE maps 12,960 lunar and Martian surface cells onto 42 environment types. Explore which Earth sites match on each dimension and see how the team would adjust an Earth test for the target conditions. The comparisons can guide what to investigate in the field.

HOW TO PLAY

Begin with
a world.

  1. 01

    Choose a world

    Select Mars or the Moon to bring it into focus. Select the same world again or choose Explore to open its surface map.

  2. 02

    Choose a surface region

    Drag the globe to rotate it and use + or − to zoom. Select a surface cell to see its coordinates and environment. Explore another way lets you enter coordinates or locate a catalogued pit. When you have the region you want, select Discover Earth analogs.

  3. 03

    Find an Earth analog

    Select a candidate in the list or a marker on Earth to locate it. Use Refine comparison to choose which dimensions determine candidate order. Select Investigate site below the candidate to open its report.

  4. 04

    Investigate the site

    View the satellite image and the strongest and weakest matches. Select All six dimensions for the full comparison. A higher similarity (from 0 to 1) means a closer relative match on that dimension. Expand the monthly test windows, engineering scenario, interpretation and cave comparisons for details where available.

Research data and methods

  • Challenge brief Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars.
  • Earth analog comparisons The team's research dataset compares Earth sites with Mars and Moon environment types across six dimensions. Natural Earth Admin 1 boundaries support regional place labels.
  • Similarity percentiles Each dimension uses its own distribution of recorded distances, including ties. Pairs with both water-ice proxies at or below 0.05 contribute to that distribution and display as “No positive ice evidence.”
  • Monthly test windows NASA POWER modelled monthly climatology for 2016–2025. Window thresholds use the team's planetary environment types. Solar geometry is calculated from latitude and season.
  • Engineering scenarios The team's November 2026 Mission IT-Π full-pool release supplies one primary scenario for each of the 3,347 Earth–planet pairs. Rover mobility draws on one JPL reference run. The other five tasks use team-built synthetic physics models.
  • Caves and pits The team shortlist draws on the LROC Pits Atlas and Mars Global Cave Candidate Catalog. Earth cave records include data © OpenStreetMap contributors under the ODbL.

How the study works

Planetary coverage and comparisons
  • Environment types Terrain fingerprints and environmental features are standardized using their median and interquartile range. Farthest-first selection adds representative cells until the largest standardized feature difference between any cell and its nearest representative is at most 1.5. All 12,960 cells remain individually selectable.
  • Earth analogs The team compares feature distributions with Wasserstein and multivariate energy distances and uses normalized differences for the environmental proxies. The study keeps each dimension separate. Its shortlist retains Pareto candidates, sites with a smaller worst-dimension distance and sites that lead on an individual dimension.
  • Reading similarity For a given dimension, similarity is the share, from 0 to 1, of recorded study distances at least as large as this pair's distance, including ties. The comparison view leaves missing values and inapplicable dimensions marked as such.
  • Research context Hipkin et al. describe how analog studies support Mars science and mission planning. Analogue sites for Mars missions.
Engineering scale transfer
  • Task assignment The smallest eligible dimension distance selects the primary task. Terrain leads to rover mobility, surface material to drilling, thermal to thermal control, illumination to power, water ice to ice operations and dust to dust mitigation.
  • Adjustment search The model samples 7,000 fixed-seed combinations of four log control multipliers, each within ±1.4. It keeps the tested setting with the lowest objective. The objective adds the largest absolute log ratio of Earth and target Π groups to 0.015 times the sum of the absolute log multipliers. An increase of 40% means 1.40 times the original Earth test setting.
  • Residual Π mismatch This is the largest absolute log-ratio remaining for the selected adjustment, before adding the control penalty. It describes the scenario's dimensionless groups. Values use assumed inputs and cannot establish field conditions or certify a test setting.
  • Method reference The team's approach is inspired by the information-theoretic treatment of dimensionless groups in Yuan & Lozano-Durán (2025), Dimensionless learning based on information.
Engineering test references
  • Rover mobility One exploratory JPL duracrust run informed the rover model. Lightholder et al., Barefoot Rover BST110 Material Data.
  • Drilling & sampling Rotary-drilling specific energy motivated the synthetic model's force and torque terms. Teale, The concept of specific energy in rock drilling.
  • Thermal control Context for conduction and radiation in the team's heat-transfer model. NASA SmallSat Institute, Thermal Control.
  • Power continuity Context for the team's solar generation and dark-period storage model. NASA SmallSat Institute, Power Subsystems.
  • Ice resource operations Related experimental work on water capture under vacuum. Krause et al., Thermal Model of Ice Growth in Vacuum for Lunar Water Production.
  • Dust mitigation Lunar dust background for the team's particle-transport model. Electrostatic effects are outside the model's scope. NASA, Lunar Dust Mitigation: A Guide and Reference.
